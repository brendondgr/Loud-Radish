Today I want to talk about why kelp forests recover faster than coral reefs after a heat wave. A kelp plant can grow half a metre in a single day under good conditions, so a forest that is wiped out in one summer can return within two or three years, provided the sea urchins are kept in check. Urchins are the key. When their predators disappear, urchins graze the young kelp down to bare rock, and you get what divers call an urchin barren. Those barrens can persist for decades, even after the water cools. So the practical lesson for restoration is not to plant kelp first. Remove the urchins first, or bring back the sea otters and sunflower stars that eat them, and the kelp largely replants itself from spores drifting in from nearby forests.
