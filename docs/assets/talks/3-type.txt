Welcome back to the show. This week we are talking about the humble typewriter, and specifically why the keys are arranged the way they are. The usual story is that the layout was designed to slow typists down so the metal arms would not jam. That story is mostly a myth. The early arrangement was shaped by telegraph operators, who transcribed Morse code and wanted letters that are often confused in Morse to sit near each other. Over a few years of small changes, driven by salesmen, operators and the factory itself, the layout settled into the one we still use. Then the typewriter companies merged, the layout became a standard, and by the time anyone proposed a better design, millions of people had already learned this one. Habit, it turns out, is the strongest engineering constraint of all.
