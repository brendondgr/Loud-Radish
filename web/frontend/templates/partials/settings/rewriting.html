{# Rewriting: what the language model is told to do with transcribed speech (D-068). Two passes hand a piece of transcription to a model and ask it to rewrite it, and until now both were driven by an instruction list compiled into the source. Those instructions are judgements — how much filler to remove, whether spoken punctuation becomes real punctuation, whether a passage stays one paragraph — and whose talk it is decides which judgement is right. Legal dictation wants the hesitations kept. A code walkthrough wants far more expansion of spoken identifiers than the shipped list grants. An empty box means the instructions that shipped, which is not the same as a box holding a copy of them: an installation that never edits one keeps tracking whatever ships next. The shipped text is a placeholder, served by the backend, and Reset writes an empty string rather than writing that text back. The guard switches are here for a reason that is easy to miss. Each guard enforces one shipped instruction after the fact, because models comply unevenly — and each will therefore quietly reverse the same instruction once somebody rewrites it. A rule asking for paragraphs produces none at all while paragraph collapsing is on, and nothing on the page would explain why. #}

Tidy a dictation

A separate pass, deliberately narrower by default. The words are yours rather than a speaker's, and they go straight into whatever window has focus, so the shipped instructions cover punctuation and obvious stumbles and nothing else.
